# الدامة / الضامة — Dhama / Dama (Arabian orthogonal draughts)

**Status:** research draft, waiting for product-owner decisions (see §4).
**Researched:** 2026-10, web research only. Some primary sources (Murray 1952, Lane 1836, Van der Stoep 1984, the TÜDAF rulebook PDF) could not be opened in full; claims taken from them through secondary summaries are marked *(via [n])*.

**Summary.** In the Gulf (Kuwait, Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia) and the Levant, *Dama / الدامة / الداما / الضامة* today means the **orthogonal draughts game** known internationally as **Turkish draughts (Türk daması)**: an 8×8 uncoloured board, 16 men a side on the second and third rows, men step and capture **forward or sideways** (never backwards or diagonally), captures are compulsory with a **maximum-capture** rule, and a man reaching the far row becomes a long-moving king (**داما**, **شيخ الدامة** / "sheikh", or **ملك**). Gulf heritage sources describe the board, pieces, movement and promotion consistently with this game [9][10][11][12][13][14], and a Qatari heritage source says it is believed to have reached the Gulf with the Ottomans [12]. Detailed written rules (multi-capture turns, draws) exist only for the Turkish federation game [6][7], so this document uses the Turkish federation rules as the precise baseline and lists every point where Gulf sources differ or are silent.

## 1. History

### 1.1 Al-qirq (القرق) and Alquerque
- The 10th-century *Kitāb al-Aghānī* of Abū al-Faraj al-Iṣfahānī names a game **al-qirq / qirkat (القرق)**; it gives no rules [1][3] *(via [17][18])*. Murray notes that *qirq* was also used for a morris-type game, so the Aghānī mention cannot be tied with certainty to the jumping game [1] *(via [18])*.
- The first surviving rules for a jumping game of this family are in Alfonso X's *Libro de los juegos* (Toledo, 1283) under the name **alquerque** (from Arabic *al-qirq*): 12 pieces a side on the 25 points of a 5×5 lined board, capture by short jump, multiple jumps allowed [17][18]. Whether capture was compulsory is unclear from the text [18].
- Van der Stoep argues from the Arabic name records that draughts-type and morris games were played in the Arab world early [4]. The common claim that European draughts descends from alquerque played on a chessboard is a standard view [1][4], but historians debate it [5].

### 1.2 The orthogonal family
- Draughts on all 64 squares of an **uncheckered** board with orthogonal moves is a distinct family: **Turkish Dama** (Türk daması), **Greek Dama** (Ntama), **Armenian Tama** (which also allows forward-diagonal steps) and the Gulf/Levant **Dama** [5][15][16]. Christian Freeling's modern *Dameo* is a 20th-century invention, not a traditional game [16].
- European travellers first describe "Turkish" draughts in the second half of the 17th century: Thévenot (1663), Mallet (1668) and Hyde (1694), the last two with some rules [5]. Pratesi warns that "dama turcica" in old texts sometimes means mancala or chess, and argues Turkish draughts is a survivor of draughts played before the board was chequered, not a derivative of European draughts [5]. Ludii (citing Murray 1951/52, p. 82) gives the same rules as today and the same late-17th-century first record [2].
- The orthogonal game is played in Turkey, Greece, Egypt, Kuwait, Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and elsewhere around the eastern Mediterranean and Middle East [15][2]. (Wikipedia tags this list "citation needed"; Kuwait and Qatar are confirmed separately below.)

### 1.3 In the Arab world
- **Egypt.** E.W. Lane (1836) lists *dámeh* (draughts) with chess (*satreng*) and backgammon (*ṭáwulah*) as favourite Egyptian pastimes, played while smoking and talking [3] *(via search excerpts)*. We could not find a rules description by Lane for *dámeh*, so Lane does **not** tell us which draughts (diagonal or orthogonal) was meant.
- **Kuwait.** KUNA (Kuwait News Agency, 2018) calls الدامة one of the oldest folk games of Kuwait, played in cafés (المقاهي), majlis gatherings (المجالس) and on pearling and trading ships; boards were squares drawn in sand, on wood, or in charcoal on concrete, with pebbles in the desert and sea-snail shells on the coast as pieces [9]. A game between experts can last an hour or more [9]. A Kuwaiti player who led a team at a Qatar Dama championship called it a mind sport to be saved from disappearing [10]. Kuwait Times describes two colours of small round wooden pieces on a board or a square cloth [11]; a Kuwaiti heritage forum gives the cloth as about 60 cm a side, 8×8 [10b].
- **Qatar.** A Dama majlis (مجلس الدامة) was revived in **Souq Waqif** (Doha) around 2009–2010 by Mohammed Saad Al-Sulaiti with support from the Amiri Diwan; it holds local and regional championships and school courses [12][12b][12c]. Qatari heritage events (Darb Al-Saai, Katara, Qatar Sports Club youth majlis) feature it [12][13]. Al-Raya reports the view that the game reached the Gulf with the Ottomans [12]. Al-Sharq says a crowned piece is a **stacked piece** (قطع مركبة) called **ملك** [12c].
- **UAE.** Sharjah Heritage Days has a Dama council pavilion; Gulf News describes 64 squares, 16 wooden pieces each (called "hatba"; Arabic spelling not given), single-square steps forward or sideways, and capture by jumping when the square beyond is empty [14].
- **Saudi Arabia / Levant.** Saudipedia (state encyclopedia, secondary) describes 16 stones (حجر) each on the second and third rows with the first row empty, one-square moves forward or sideways, no retreat, capture when stones meet "face to face or side by side" with the square beyond empty, and a crowned **داما** that captures forwards and backwards at any distance when nothing is between [8]. A Palestinian heritage page describes the same game and says the player announces "داما!" on crowning, and that capture is compulsory (القتل إجباري) [8b].
- **North Africa (different games).** Morocco/Algeria **Kharbaga** (crowned piece = *Mullah*) and Saharan **Zamma / Dhamet / Srand** are alquerque-type games on lined boards, not orthogonal Dama [19][20]. They are a separate family and not a candidate for this product's Dama.

## 2. Board, pieces, starting position

- **Board:** 8×8 = 64 squares, **all squares used**, board not chequered (a plain grid; any colouring is decorative) [2][8][13][15].
- **Pieces:** 16 men per side, two colours. Light/White and Dark/Black [2][6][15]. Gulf sources: 16 each [8][13][14]. (Some Arabic web pages say 12 on dark squares; that is diagonal/English checkers confused with Dama and is rejected. Al-Raya's "32 stones each" is read as 32 in total [12].)
- **Coordinates used in this doc:** files a–h left to right from White's side, ranks 1–8 from White's side.
- **Start:** White men on all of ranks **2 and 3** (a2–h3); Black men on all of ranks **6 and 7** (a6–h7). Ranks 1 and 8 are empty [2][6][8][15].
- **Promotion row:** a White man promotes on rank 8, a Black man on rank 1.

```
8  . . . . . . . .
7  b b b b b b b b
6  b b b b b b b b
5  . . . . . . . .
4  . . . . . . . .
3  w w w w w w w w
2  w w w w w w w w
1  . . . . . . . .
   a b c d e f g h
```

## 3. Rules (baseline = Turkish federation rules, Gulf-compatible)

Directions: *forward* = towards the opponent's back rank; *sideways* = left/right along the same rank. "Orthogonal" = forward, backward, left, right. Nothing in Dama is diagonal.

- **R1 — First move.** White moves first [15][22]. *(Gulf sources are silent; TÜDAF text not checked on this point.)*
- **R2 — Turns.** Players alternate; a turn is one move (a step or one full capture sequence). Passing is not allowed.
- **R3 — Man step.** A man moves one square **forward or sideways** to an empty square. It never moves backwards or diagonally [2][6][8][14][15].
- **R4 — Man capture.** A man captures by jumping over an orthogonally adjacent enemy piece (forward or sideways, **not backwards**) to the empty square immediately beyond [2][6][8][15].
- **R5 — Multi-capture.** If after a capture the same piece can capture again, it must continue in the same turn; the sequence ends only when no further capture is available to that piece [2][6][15].
- **R6 — Capture is compulsory.** If any capture is available, the player must capture [2][6][8b][13][15].
- **R7 — Maximum capture.** Among all capture sequences available (with any piece), the player must choose one that captures the **greatest number of pieces**. Kings and men count the same (one each); there is no priority for capturing kings or for capturing with a king. If several sequences tie, the player chooses freely [2][6][15][22].
- **R8 — Immediate removal ("Turkish strike").** Each captured piece is removed **as soon as it is jumped**, not at the end of the sequence. Therefore a capturing piece may pass over a square it has emptied earlier in the same turn, and new capture lines can open mid-sequence [15][22].
- **R9 — No 180° turn.** Between two consecutive captures in one sequence, the capturing piece may continue straight or turn 90°, but may **not reverse direction (180°)** [2][6][15][22]. (For men this only matters in theory: a man cannot capture backwards, and a sideways reversal would cross the just-emptied square. It is a real restriction for kings.)
- **R10 — Promotion.** A man that ends its move on the opponent's back rank becomes a king (crowned by stacking a second piece, Gulf practice [12c]; or turned/marked). Gulf: the player announces "داما!" [8b].
  - **R10a — Promotion by a plain step:** the move ends; the piece acts as a king from its next turn [6].
  - **R10b — Promotion during a capture:** if the man reaches the back rank by capturing and can capture again, it **must continue capturing in the same turn**. TÜDAF specifies it continues **as a man** (i.e. only sideways jumps along the back rank) and gains king powers only after the opponent's move [6][7]. Several secondary sources say only that it "continues", without saying as man or king [2][15][22]. See Decision D3.
- **R11 — King move.** A king moves any number of empty squares in a straight orthogonal line (forward, backward, left, right) — a "flying" king [2][6][8][15].
- **R12 — King capture.** A king captures by moving along a line, over any number of empty squares, jumping **exactly one** enemy piece, and landing on **any** empty square beyond it on the same line (as far as the squares are empty). It may not jump two pieces in a row, nor its own piece [6][15][22]. From the landing square it continues capturing per R5, R7, R8, R9 (90° turns allowed, 180° not). If only some landing squares allow the sequence to continue, R7 forces a landing that achieves the maximum. (Jocly's rule page says a king must stop right behind the captured piece; this contradicts the federation and all other sources and is treated as an error [22].)
- **R13 — Touch-move (over-the-board etiquette).** A piece lifted must be moved [6]. Not needed in a digital game.
- **R14 — Win.** A player wins when the opponent has **no pieces** or **no legal move** on their turn (all blocked) [2][6][8][15].
- **R15 — Draws (federation).** (a) Agreement; (b) the **same position occurs three times**; (c) **each side has only one piece left** [6][7]. Gulf sources: the Jawaker online Dama app (Gulf/Levant audience) draws on one king each [21]. See Decision D5 for king vs single man.
- **R16 — No huffing.** Under the federation rules a missed capture is not a "huff" penalty; compulsory capture is simply enforced. No Arab source found mentions huffing. A digital game enforces R6–R7 automatically.

**Recommendation for a family app:** implement the **TÜDAF ruleset as "الدامة" (standard)**, because it is the only complete written rule set, it matches every rule that Gulf heritage sources actually state (board, 16 men on rows 2–3, forward/sideways men, compulsory capture, flying king), and Gulf sources describe the same game in less detail. Name any change from it as a house variant. Add a fixed move-limit draw as a clearly named engine safeguard (D6, §5) because the federation rules lack one.

### Decisions needed from product owner

1. **D1 — Base ruleset.** A) TÜDAF Turkish rules, presented as Gulf Dama (**recommended**); B) "Gulf Dama" with only what Gulf sources state, other points left as house rules; C) offer both as selectable rule sets.
2. **D2 — Maximum-capture rule.** A) Compulsory maximum by count, kings = men (**recommended**, federation); B) compulsory capture but free choice of sequence (easier for children, a named "easy" house variant); C) A plus king priority (Jawaker app only, not recommended).
3. **D3 — Promotion during a capture.** A) Continue capturing as a man, king from next turn (**recommended**, TÜDAF); B) continue as a king immediately; C) move ends on promotion.
4. **D4 — King turning.** A) 90° turns allowed, 180° forbidden (**recommended**); B) no direction change during a king's multi-capture (unclear Qatari-edition report).
5. **D5 — King vs single man / one piece each.** A) Draw when each side has one piece (**recommended**, TÜDAF); B) one king vs one man = win for the king, one piece each otherwise draw (Murray/Samara variant).
6. **D6 — Move-limit draw (engine safeguard, not attested).** A) Draw after 50 moves by each side with no capture and no man move (**recommended**, named "Liwan house rule"); B) no limit beyond 3-fold repetition.
7. **D7 — Naming in the app.** A) الدامة with subtitle "Dama" (Gulf spelling, **recommended**); B) الضامة; C) الداما. And name the king: A) شيخ (Kuwait/Qatar/UAE heritage, **recommended**); B) داما; C) ملك.
8. **D8 — Alquerque (القرق) later.** Include Alquerque as a future heritage game (rules from *Libro de los juegos*, 1283, with the al-Aghānī mention as history)? A) yes, as a later heritage game (**recommended**: it is the documented Arabic ancestor and fits the shared interface); B) only as a story in the heritage section; C) no.

## 5. Notes for implementation

- **Move generation order:** if any capture exists, generate only capture sequences; compute each sequence's length and keep only the maximum (R6, R7). A move is the full path (list of landing squares + captured squares), so the UI can animate and the server can validate it.
- **Search:** depth-first over capture steps. Remove each captured piece from the working board immediately (R8). Keep the previous direction to forbid 180° (R9). A piece can never be captured twice because it is gone after the first jump.
- **King capture branching:** after a jump, try every empty landing square beyond; each is a separate branch.
- **Promotion mid-capture (D3-A):** if a man lands on its promotion rank during a sequence, keep generating as a man (sideways only); set the "king" flag when the sequence ends.
- **Two identical paths:** two sequences with the same landing squares but different captured pieces (possible with flying kings) are different moves; deduplicate on (path, captured set).
- **Termination:** side to move with no pieces or no legal move loses. Draw on 3-fold repetition (position + side to move), on D5, and on the D6 counter. Reset the D6 counter on any capture or man move.
- **Determinism:** no randomness; first player fixed (White) unless an injected seed chooses colours.
- **RTL:** coordinates are internal; display notation must be mirrored explicitly for Arabic. The board itself is symmetric left-right, so mirroring the view changes no rule.

**Test positions** (W = white man, WK = white king, B/BK likewise; White to move unless stated):
1. Start position: each rank-3 man can step forward; all sideways steps are blocked. Expect exactly **8** legal moves.
2. W d4, B d5, d6 empty → capture d4×d6 forced; no step moves allowed.
3. W d4, B d3 (behind) → no capture (no backward capture); normal steps only.
4. W d4, B e4, B g4, f4 and h4 empty → d4×f4×h4 is one move of 2 captures (sideways chain).
5. Max rule: W a4 with B a5 (a6 empty) = 1 capture; W h4 with B h5, B g6 (h6, f6 empty) = h4×h6×f6 = 2 captures. Only the 2-capture sequence is legal.
6. King flying: WK a1, B a5, a6–a8 empty → landings a6, a7, a8 all legal (if no continuation exists from any of them).
7. King must land where continuation exists: WK a1, B a5, B c7, a2–a4, a6–a8, b7, d7–h7 empty → only landing a7 allows a7×(c7)→d7..h7; max rule forces a7 then right.
8. 180° ban: WK d4, B d6, B d2, all other d-file squares empty, nothing else on the board → legal moves are d4×d6 (landing d7 or d8) and d4×d2 (landing d1), each capturing **1**. Without R9 the king could reverse and take both; the engine must not generate that 2-capture move.
9. Immediate removal: a king path that re-crosses an emptied square to reach a third piece is legal.
10. Promotion mid-capture (D3-A): W c6, B c7, B d8, c8 and e8 empty → c6×c8 promotes; continues sideways c8×e8 as a man; ends as king.
11. Plain promotion: W c7 → c8 ends move; no capture this turn even if possible from c8 as a king.
12. Blocked loss: Black to move with all men blocked and no captures → White wins.
13. One piece each (D5-A) → draw immediately.

## 6. Notes for tutorials (learning order)

1. **The board and the pieces** — 8×8 plain grid, 16 حجر each on rows 2–3; heritage story (sand boards, shells, pearling ships) [9].
2. **Moving a man** — one step forward or sideways; never back.
3. **Capturing** — jump an adjacent piece forward or sideways; "أكل".
4. **Capture is a must** — if you can capture, you must.
5. **Chains** — keep jumping in one turn; pieces vanish as you jump (Turkish strike).
6. **Take the most** — choose the chain that captures the most pieces.
7. **Becoming a sheikh / داما** — reach the last row; crowning by stacking.
8. **The flying king** — move far, capture from a distance, land anywhere beyond; turn 90°, never back.
9. **Winning and draws** — capture all or block all; one piece each is a draw; repetition.
10. **First tactics** — sacrifice to open a king's line; keep the back row guarded.
